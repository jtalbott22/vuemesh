The tour

Every screen, in the order a show happens.

VUEMesh is distributed live production: a handful of pages, each one a job, each opening in a browser. The studio that runs the show, the QR Beacon people scan, the cameras, microphones, shared screens and calls that feed it, the tools to mix, watch and stream it, the screens that show it, and the edit afterwards. Here's what each one looks like.

  • Set up
  • Bring in sources
  • Run the show
  • Show it
  • Afterwards

Set up

On a laptop or desktop

The studio

The control room. Every camera and microphone as a tile, the program on air, and a switcher with CUT, AUTO and a T-bar.

  • Cut it yourself, or let the Auto Director
  • Break and black, lower thirds, clips
  • Record the program, stream it live
Start a show

On any screen

The QR Beacon

One code on a screen. A phone scans it to join, and while the camera sees it, it reads the time inside, so every angle lines up to the frame later.

  • Put it on a TV, a spare laptop or a tablet
  • Shows who's in and who's recording

On a computer, a stick PC on a TV, or a newer tablet

The kiosk

A screen that runs a whole show by itself. It shows its code; guests scan in with their phones, and it directs and records with nobody at the controls.

Open a kiosk

Bring in sources

On a phone, a tablet or a laptop

The camera

Scan, tap, type a name: it's a camera in the show. It records itself in full quality and sends the file to the studio, while the studio sees a live picture.

  • Zoom, lens, exposure and focus
  • A tally light when it's on air
  • Messages from the director
Try the camera

On a phone or a tablet

The microphone

Any phone can be a microphone instead: set it down near the voice, or plug in a USB mic, and it records clean sound for the show.

  • Its own level meter
  • Pick which microphone it uses

On the presenter's computer

Share a screen

A presenter opens /share, types the show's code and picks a screen, window or tab. It arrives in the studio as a source over the local network, sharp for slides and smooth for video, with its sound.

  • A tally tells them when they're up next or on air
  • Nothing to install, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari
Open /share

From anywhere, on any device

Calls

Up to six people on cameras and shared screens, everyone connected straight to everyone. Send a link, or a code and PIN; nobody gets in until someone in the call lets them.

  • The studio can join: each person becomes a source in the show
  • Captions made on each device, and nothing recorded by the call
Start a call

Run the show

In the studio, or its own window

The audio mixer

A strip for every source: audio follows video, always on or off, a fader, EQ, balance and mono or stereo, with stereo meters for the program.

  • Pop out the audio console for spectrum and phase

In its own window

Camera control

Every camera side by side with a histogram and waveform, and its zoom, exposure, contrast and focus, set from here. Click the picture to focus there.

In its own window

The monitor

The health of the show at a glance: each source's network, battery and bitrate, what's coming in and going out, and the event log.

On your phone

The remote

Pair a phone and run the show from anywhere in the room: cut cameras, call a break, fade to black, start the recording.

Show it

In the studio

Go live

Stream out sends the program to a streaming service. WHIP services such as Twitch take it straight from the browser; RTMP services such as YouTube, Facebook, Kick and Vimeo go through a small relay on the studio computer. Pick the service, paste the key, press Go live.

  • The key stays on the studio computer
  • Program out, for a projector or OBS, and the program as a camera in Zoom, Teams or Meet

On a TV, a projector, or any browser

TV screens

Open /tv, enter the show's code, and once the studio lets it in it shows the live program straight from the studio, browses the night's recordings, or becomes the QR Beacon.

Open /tv

Afterwards

In its own window, beside the studio

Edit

When the full-quality recordings arrive, the live cut is already on the timeline, every clip lined up on one clock. Change an angle, trim, add titles, then render an MP4.

  • Every angle in Angles, with Source and Program monitors
  • Ripple, roll, slip and slide, with Premiere Pro's keys
  • Render MP4 faster than real time

On every device that recorded

My media

Everything this device recorded, kept on the device: play it, download it, share it, or delete it. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
