VUEMesh, OBS Studio and VDO.Ninja

Three free tools that people often weigh up for live video. They overlap less than it seems, and they work well together. Here's what each one is for, in plain words first and then in detail.

Updated 15 November 2026 (VUEMesh 0.93.0). What this page says about OBS Studio and VDO.Ninja comes from their own documentation and may have changed since: check obsproject.com and docs.vdo.ninja, and tell us if something here is out of date.

In one line each

  • VUEMeshA whole production studio in a browser tab: switcher, audio mixer, recorder, live streamer and video editor. Phones join as cameras by scanning a QR code.
  • OBS StudioA desktop app for building scenes, recording and streaming from one computer. The deepest control over layouts, encoding and plugins.
  • VDO.NinjaA browser tool that sends live cameras and guests from one device to another over the internet, usually into OBS or similar software.

Which to choose

Choose VUEMesh when

  • You want several angles of an event (a wedding, a gig, a game, a service) from the phones and tablets people already have, with nothing to install.
  • You want every angle kept in full quality and the live cut ready to re-edit.
  • One laptop with USB cameras and a screen share should run a talk, a podcast or a class.
  • Someone new has to run it, or the show should run itself.

Choose OBS Studio when

  • You need complex layouts, overlays and scenes, or a large plugin ecosystem.
  • You work with NDI, SDI or a rack of capture cards, or a dedicated streaming PC.
  • You want fine control of encoders, SRT, or several outputs at once.

Choose VDO.Ninja when

  • You bring remote guests or off-site cameras into a show that's already built in OBS, vMix or similar.
  • Low latency over the internet matters more than everything being in one place.
  • You're comfortable tuning it with its link options.

Using them together

  • VUEMesh into OBS: Program out opens VUEMesh's program in a window of its own, which OBS can capture like any window. VUEMesh does the cameras, switching and recording; OBS adds its graphics and encoding.
  • VUEMesh as a webcam: the VUEMesh relay sends the program to OBS's virtual camera (on a Mac or Windows), so it's a camera in Zoom, Teams or Meet. On Linux the relay makes the camera itself.
  • Guests: for remote guests, VUEMesh has its own video calls of up to 6 people; a show already built around VDO.Ninja and OBS can capture VUEMesh's program window beside it.

What VUEMesh doesn't do

  • The studio needs a computer. A phone can be a camera, a microphone or a remote, but not the studio.
  • It streams to one service at a time. To reach several platforms at once, stream to a multistreaming service such as Restream.
  • RTMP services (YouTube, Facebook, Kick, Vimeo) need the free VUEMesh relay on the studio computer, because a browser can't send RTMP itself. WHIP services (Twitch, Restream, Cloudflare Stream) don't.
  • No NDI or SDI inputs: sources are what a browser can reach. An HDMI capture device that shows up as a webcam works like one.
  • People watching from elsewhere see the live stream, not each separate angle.
  • Venue Wi-Fi that keeps devices from seeing each other needs a travel router, or the studio computer's own hotspot.
  • The rest is in the known limits.
