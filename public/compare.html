How VUEMesh compares

People weigh VUEMesh against apps that switch phones as cameras, browser studios for guests, and desktop streaming software. VUEMesh is a different model, distributed live production: instead of one machine with every camera cabled in, the cameras are the phones, laptops and webcams already there, connected browser to browser, and every one records itself on the same clock. Here it is beside the three it's most often compared with, then how it differs from the rest. Plain words first, detail after.

Which to choose

Choose VUEMesh when

  • Several angles of an event (a wedding, a gig, a game, a service) from the phones and cameras people already have, with nothing to install.
  • Every angle kept in full quality, and the live cut ready to re-edit.
  • One laptop with USB cameras and a screen share for a talk, a podcast or a class, on Mac or Windows.
  • Free, with no account, and video that stays on your devices.

Choose Switcher Studio when

  • Your team is all on iPhones and iPads and wants an app.
  • Streaming to several platforms at once, and selling or hosting video, matter most.

Choose StreamYard when

  • Your show is mostly remote guests and interviews.
  • You want branding and comments on screen, and cloud storage, in a browser.

Choose OBS Studio when

  • You want complex layouts and a large plugin ecosystem on one computer.
  • You use NDI, capture cards or fine encoder control, or several outputs at once.

Using them together

  • VUEMesh into OBS, vMix or Ecamm: Program out opens VUEMesh's program in a window of its own, which they can capture like any window. VUEMesh does the cameras, switching and recording; they add their graphics and encoding.
  • VUEMesh as a webcam: the VUEMesh relay sends the program to OBS's virtual camera (on a Mac or Windows), so it's a camera in Zoom, Teams, Meet, or a browser studio such as StreamYard. On Linux the relay makes the camera itself.
  • To several platforms at once: stream VUEMesh to a multistreaming service such as Restream, which passes it on.

What VUEMesh doesn't do

  • The studio needs a computer. A phone can be a camera, a microphone or a remote, but not the studio.
  • It streams to one service at a time. To reach several platforms at once, stream to a multistreaming service such as Restream.
  • RTMP services (YouTube, Facebook, Kick, Vimeo) need the free VUEMesh relay on the studio computer, because a browser can't send RTMP itself. WHIP services (Twitch, Restream, Cloudflare Stream) don't.
  • No NDI or SDI inputs: sources are what a browser can reach. An HDMI capture device that shows up as a webcam works like one.
  • No cloud storage or video hosting: recordings stay on your devices, and you keep or share the files yourself.
  • People watching from elsewhere see the live stream, not each separate angle.
  • Venue Wi-Fi that keeps devices from seeing each other needs a travel router, or the studio computer's own hotspot.
  • The rest is in the known limits.

Technical detail

For a broadcast or AV desk: what VUEMesh does under the hood.

Output720p or 1080p, landscape or portrait; 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50 or 60 frames a second
TransportWebRTC, device to device over the local network; STUN, and a TURN relay only when there's no other path (media stays encrypted end to end)
SyncOne clock on the studio computer; phones read an optical clock off the QR Beacon through their cameras; sound sync on shared audio; frame-accurate, no timecode hardware
RecordingProgram recorded live; every source on its own at full quality (phones record locally and deliver each take, signed and verified part by part); captions as WebVTT
StreamingWHIP from the browser; RTMP and RTMPS through a local relay that re-encodes to H.264 (keyframe every 2 s) and AAC at 160 kbit/s; 2.5 to 6 Mbit/s
RenderingMP4 with hardware H.264 in Chrome and Edge, faster than real time; Linux records VP9
CapacityAbout 12 live pictures on a typical laptop at 720p on a good Wi-Fi 6 network; 6 to 8 on older Wi-Fi, fewer at 1080p or on a slower computer. Phones beyond that still record, and swap in when there's room. The Monitor shows what's holding each picture back: the network or the phone
ControlKeyboard shortcuts, a MIDI fader as the T-bar, a paired phone remote, and Premiere Pro's keys in Edit
PrivacyNo accounts, cookies or third-party trackers; recordings stay on your devices; event data deletes itself after 30 days
