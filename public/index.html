A production system.

Nothing you shoot is uploaded to be processed somewhere else. VUEMesh coordinates your cameras, microphones and controls in the browser; the cloud only introduces your devices to each other, and the pictures go straight between them on your own network.

  • Browser-native No app to install. A compatible browser is the production client.

One browser coordinates the production.

The beacon gives each camera a way into the same session and a shared clock. A phone, laptop or other browser-capable camera can join without installing an app, becoming another source in the production.

Nothing to install.

A browser, cameras, microphones and a network. That's the point. VUEMesh turns the devices you already have into a coordinated production system — without asking anyone to install an app.

  • A network that connects the sources The production sources need to communicate reliably. A solid local network is more important than a huge internet connection. Venue Wi-Fi can work. A network you control is the safest bet for production.
  • A browser to run the studio Open the studio in a browser and it becomes the control room: camera views, sound, program output, director controls and recording. Chrome or Edge on a suitable computer is the simplest way to run the studio.
  • Then the cameras show up Phones become cameras, laptops can provide cameras and microphones, and dedicated devices can become fixed angles you control. Multiple microphones can participate too. Use the sources you already have.

VUEMesh uses the cloud to coordinate sessions and connect participants. The production is designed to happen between the browsers and the host rather than requiring every video stream to pass through a central video server.

Then make it a real control room.

One computer can run the show. Add screens, dedicated cameras, microphones or a public display and the same browser-native system scales into a full production environment.

  • Direct from your pocket Pair a phone as a remote and walk the room: cut cameras, call a break, fade to black, start the recording, all while you keep an eye on the real thing. The laptop runs the show. You don't have to stand next to it.
  • Every screen in the room is a monitor Open /tv on a smart TV, a spare laptop or a projector, and it shows the live program straight from the studio, browses the night's recordings, or becomes the beacon guests scan. Built for the big screen, and coming to more of them.
  • More screens, more control Put the program on one display, the beacon on another, and keep camera control, sound and monitoring in their own windows. The browser becomes the control room. One screen is enough to start. More screens turn the same system into a larger production desk.
  • Go live anywhere Send the finished program to YouTube, Facebook, Twitch, Kick, Vimeo, LinkedIn, X, or another service that accepts RTMP or WHIP. The studio guides you through the connection. Then it's paste your stream key, press Go live.

Podcasts. Shows. Games. Classrooms. Anywhere you need more cameras without a bigger truck.
